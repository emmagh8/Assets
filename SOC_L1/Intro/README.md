## Why Do We Need a SOC?

Today, almost every organization relies on the internet, cloud services, and digital systems to store and process large amounts of data. A single mistake, such as clicking a malicious link or opening an infected attachment, can lead to a serious security incident.

Because of these risks, organizations need a Security Operations Center (SOC) to:

* Monitor security events and activities
* Detect malicious behavior
* Investigate security incidents
* Prevent data breaches
* Protect critical systems and sensitive information

The SOC serves as the organization's first line of defense against cyber threats.

---

## Responsibilities of a Junior Security Analyst

A Junior Security Analyst is responsible for:

* Monitoring and investigating security alerts
* Identifying suspicious activities
* Triaging alerts and escalating complex cases to Level 2 analysts
* Learning about new attack techniques and defensive measures
* Supporting incident detection and response activities

## Why Are People Targeted in Cyber Attacks?

Humans are often considered the weakest link in cybersecurity. Attackers frequently target employees because they can provide access to:

* Company websites
* Email accounts
* Internal systems
* Databases and sensitive information

Instead of attacking technology directly, attackers often manipulate people through social engineering.

Social engineering is a technique used to trick individuals into revealing sensitive information or performing actions that benefit an attacker. Common emotions exploited by attackers include:

* Fear
* Urgency
* Curiosity
* Trust

### Example: Phishing Attack

An employee in the HR department receives an email stating:

> "A new login to your Google account was detected from a Galaxy S24. Click here to verify the activity."

The message creates a sense of urgency and directs the victim to a fake login page that appears legitimate. If the victim enters their credentials, the attacker gains access to the username and password.

Other social engineering techniques include:

* Malicious attachments
* Fake software downloads
* Deepfake-based scams
* SMS phishing (Smishing)
* Voice phishing (Vishing)

---

## How Does the SOC Defend Against Social Engineering?

SOC teams help reduce the risk of social engineering attacks through a combination of technology, awareness, and incident response.

### 1. Security Technologies

* Anti-phishing solutions
* Antivirus and Endpoint Detection & Response (EDR) tools
* Email security gateways
* Web filtering solutions

### 2. Security Awareness Training

Employees are trained to:

* Recognize phishing emails
* Verify suspicious requests
* Report potential security incidents
* Follow security best practices

### 3. Monitoring and Incident Response

SOC analysts investigate reported incidents, contain threats, and help prevent further compromise.

## How Do Attackers Exploit Vulnerable Systems?

Attackers often target vulnerable or misconfigured systems to:

* Steal sensitive information
* Deploy ransomware
* Gain unauthorized access
* Disrupt business operations
* Destroy critical data

Common attack vectors include:

* Unpatched software
* Weak passwords
* Misconfigured services
* Malicious USB devices
* Malware infections

---

## How SOC Analysts Protect Systems

SOC analysts help defend systems by:

* Monitoring network and endpoint activity
* Detecting suspicious behavior
* Applying security patches
* Blocking malicious IP addresses
* Strengthening access controls
* Enforcing strong password policies
* Investigating and responding to incidents

Their goal is to reduce risk and minimize the impact of cyber attacks.

The Blue Team focuses on defensive security. Its primary objective is to protect an organization's systems, networks, and data from cyber threats.
